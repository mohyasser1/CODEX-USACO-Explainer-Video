TEACHING CONTRACT
Deep dive = contract and prerequisites; one continuous numerical trace;
state/invariant; correctness argument; time and auxiliary space; failing
alternative; complete runnable program; transfer exercise and separate answer.
Introduction = mechanism and one trace, explicitly not implementation mastery.
Roadmap = when useful, prerequisites and follow-up practice; no mastery claim.
Division names describe approximate learning groups, not an official syllabus.

FOUR-PART MINUTE BUDGET (240 minutes; targets, not measured audio yet)
Each part includes brief retrieval prompts and answers within these budgets.
Long coding time is learner-controlled pausing, not padded silence.

PART 1 — FROM SOURCE CODE TO A CORRECT BRONZE SOLUTION (60 min)
  0–5   Tour contract, compile/run/input/output, errors and debugging loop.
  5–15  Types, expressions, overflow before assignment, signed arithmetic;
         statement-by-statement execution and memory-value diagrams.
 15–27  Conditions/loops, vectors/string indices, boundaries; signed-total
         deep dive with empty/negative inputs and a loop invariant.
 27–35  Functions, value/reference, scope, call stack, vector size/capacity;
         complexity and why a reference can be invalidated by reallocation.
 35–47  Simulation plus exhaustive casework: three possible starting states;
         full problem-to-code derivation and tests (Shell Game follow-up).
 47–57  Backtracking subsets: choose/recurse/undo, leaf coverage, exponential
         cost; subset-sum trace and why premature pruning fails with negatives.
 57–60  Retrieval, transfer solution, practice route and build checklist.

PART 2 — MAKE REPEATED WORK DISAPPEAR (60 min)
  0–8   Sorting, comparators, pairs, maps/sets; order versus physical distance.
  8–20  Prefix sums deep dive: half-open indexing, cancellation proof, counts.
 20–27  Difference-array reconstruction and numerical 2D inclusion–exclusion
         introductions; full implementations in companion exercises.
 27–37  Sorted two-sum deep dive; discard proof, overflow; nonnegative window
         introduction with a negative-value counterexample.
 37–52  Binary search on truck capacity deep dive, including greedy predicate
         proof, infeasible capacities, feasible bound and shrinking interval.
 52–58  Interval scheduling greedy introduction and exchange proof; wrong
         duration-first and earliest-start alternatives.
 58–60  Retrieval/transfer and official Breed Counting practice link.

PART 3 — RELATIONSHIPS AND REUSABLE SUBPROBLEMS (60 min)
  0–8   Graph models, directed/undirected, adjacency lists, disconnected cases.
  8–21  BFS deep dive: evolving queue, mark-on-enqueue, distances and parents;
         DFS/flood-fill comparison and recursion-depth risk.
 21–33  Parity graph constraints deep dive: same/different edges, propagation,
         contradiction and components; Great Revegetation follow-up.
 33–39  Functional graphs introduction: tail/cycle, visited states and repeated
         successors; binary jumping linked to part 4's roadmap.
 39–55  DP deep dive: minimum coins, state/transition/base/unreachable/order,
         bottom-up trace, optimal-substructure proof, greedy counterexample.
 55–60  DAG/topological order introduction: dependencies, cycle detection;
         transfer solutions and graph/DP practice plan.

PART 4 — GOLD FOUNDATIONS AND THE ADVANCED MAP (60 min)
  0–12  0/1 knapsack deep dive: two-dimensional state then descending capacity;
         show ascending update accidentally reuses an item.
 12–23  Dijkstra deep dive: nonnegative weights, stale entries, relaxation,
         priority queue, finalized-distance proof; negative-edge counterexample.
 23–30  DSU/Kruskal introduction: components, path compression, union by size,
         sorted edges, cycle rejection and cut-property explanation.
 30–42  Fenwick deep dive: binary lowbit blocks, prefix query, point addition,
         interval answer and an update trace; static-prefix contrast.
 42–49  Segment tree introduction: associative merge, identity, query/update;
         sparse-table static idempotent RMQ contrast and limitations.
 49–57  Prerequisite-organized roadmap: Euler tour/LCA/tree DP; LIS/interval DP;
         sieve/GCD/modular arithmetic/bitmask/MITM; SCC/bridges/Eulerian walks;
         lazy/persistent structures; strings/flow/geometry/DP optimization.
         These are introductions or named follow-ups, not fully taught units.
 57–60  Mixed technique-selection exercise, test oracle method, training plan.

ENTERTAINMENT / VISUAL DESIGN
Rohail, a fictional tenth grader, helps Maya and Sam organize a school music
night. Use believable ticket refunds, rehearsal choices, group constraints and
equipment budgets. Large contest bounds are hypothetical stress tests, not
claims about actual school attendance. No cow or mascot in active V2.
Every joke must reveal a misconception or motivate a state change. Queue
animation is an entry line; priority is distance, not follower count.
Backtracking is a set-list audition with visible undo. Warm original synthetic
narration requires a listening audition; the earlier flat voice was rejected.
Original rap transitions recap an invariant in plain language immediately after.
Song titles and factual high-level Drake–Kendrick rivalry references can frame
competition. Selected clean user-supplied excerpts may be adapted as short
pedagogical hooks with original explanations; no additional lyric retrieval,
recordings, melody copying, artist voice imitation or implied endorsement.
